Deep Research — Thread 2 of 4: Volume Ruler (Hard Sets, MEV/MRV, Progression) for Helm
Executive Summary
The Helm Volume Ruler provides a deterministic computational framework for tracking, allocating, and progressing training volume for muscle hypertrophy. Hypertrophy engines often suffer from two operational flaws: subjective volume modifications based on anecdotal user feedback and binary set accounting that either overcounts synergist movements—leading to under-prescribed direct work—or ignores them entirely, inducing systemic overtraining. The system specified herein resolves these flaws by implementing an offline-first calculation engine within PlanKit that treats training volume as an explicit ledger of direct and fractional hard sets.
By establishing strict mathematical boundaries for what constitutes a hard set, mapping compound exercise muscle contributions through fractional coefficients, and regulating weekly targets via an autoregulated Minimum Effective Volume (MEV) to Maximum Recoverable Volume (MRV) state machine, PlanKit generates testable exercise prescriptions. The Large Language Model (LLM) conversational layer functions strictly as an interface to explain ledger state and propose bounded adaptations. All numerical modifications are calculated and clamped by the deterministic rules of the PlanKit core engine.
Hard-Set Definition and Counting Rules
Primary Definition of a Hard Set
A hard set is defined within PlanKit as any completed exercise set executed within a load intensity of $30\%\text{--}85\%$ of one-repetition maximum ($1\text{RM}$), across a rep range of $5\text{--}30$ repetitions, terminating at a proximity to failure of $0\text{--}4$ Reps in Reserve ($\text{RIR}$) (or Rating of Perceived Exertion ($\text{RPE}$) $6\text{--}10$). Sets executed below $30\% \text{ 1RM}$, at reps exceeding 30, or with an $\text{RIR} > 4$ fail to generate sufficient mechanical tension or motor unit recruitment across high-threshold motor units to drive hypertrophic adaptations. Consequently, such sets are assigned a value of $0.00$ hard sets in the volume ledger.

Accounting for Intensity Techniques and Overlapping Slots
When logging specialized intensity techniques, PlanKit enforces a maximum set credit cap per exercise entry to prevent arbitrary volume inflation. A single exercise slot utilizing rest-pause or drop sets cannot exceed $1.50$ total effective sets toward the primary target muscle.
For multi-slot exercise configurations occurring within a single workout session (such as two distinct back pulling movements performed during a single Pull session), each completed working set credits the primary target muscle fully. However, to prevent acute intra-session fatigue from generating unproductive "junk volume," total primary set volume per muscle group is capped at $8.0\text{--}10.0$ effective sets per session. Any working set completed beyond $10.0$ effective sets for a single muscle group in one session yields a decayed stimulus credit of $0.50$ effective sets in the weekly ledger, reflecting intra-session diminishing returns.
Muscle Contribution Model
Fractional Set Accounting Framework
Traditional binary set counting—assigning $1.0$ set to all involved muscles or $1.0$ set only to the primary muscle—introduces systemic programming errors. Binary counting either over-prescribes secondary isolation work or omits the substantial hypertrophic stimulus received by synergist muscles during heavy compound movements.
PlanKit utilizes a fractional set mapping model supported by multi-level meta-regressions. Compound movements assign primary movers $1.00$ set credit, major synergists $0.50$ set credit, and minor stabilisers $0.25$ or $0.00$ set credit.
$$\text{Effective Weekly Volume } (V_{\text{eff}, m}) = \sum S_{\text{direct}, m} + \sum \left( \omega_{i, m} \cdot S_{\text{indirect}, i} \right)$$
In this equation, $S_{\text{direct}, m}$ represents the count of direct working sets for muscle group $m$, $S_{\text{indirect}, i}$ represents the count of working sets performed on compound exercise $i$, and $\omega_{i, m} \in \{0.50, 0.25\}$ defines the primary-to-synergist contribution coefficient.

Synergist Caps and Target Protection Rules
To prevent fractional volume from masking a complete lack of direct isolation stimulus (for example, performing 20 sets of heavy rows and accumulating $10.0$ fractional sets for biceps without executing a direct curl), PlanKit enforces two deterministic rules:
Synergist Contribution Ceiling: Indirect fractional sets ($\sum \omega_{i, m} \cdot S_{\text{indirect}, i}$) can satisfy a maximum of $50\%$ of a muscle group's total weekly Target Volume ($V_{\text{target}}$). The remaining $50\%$ must be supplied by direct isolation or primary mover sets ($S_{\text{direct}} = 1.00$).
Direct Floor Minimum: If a muscle group's weekly MEV target is $\ge 8.0$ sets, PlanKit mandates a hard minimum floor of at least $4.0$ direct sets ($S_{\text{direct}}$) per week allocated to dedicated primary pattern slots.

Global scalar multipliers (such as multiplying all muscle groups by $0.85$ for novices) introduce errors because smaller muscle groups and intermediate lifters exhibit non-linear volume tolerances.
PlanKit establishes muscle-specific baseline volume landmarks (measured in total effective weekly sets) for intermediate lifters, applying demographic modifiers based on empirical recovery capacity.

Mid-Block Reseed Policy
When a user modifies profile parameters (such as changing experience level or training template) mid-mesocycle, PlanKit executes a non-destructive landmark recalculation:
Completed sets and historical week targets within the active mesocycle remain unchanged in the ledger to preserve historical tracking. Uncompleted weekly targets ($V_{\text{target}, w}$) for remaining weeks $w \in \{w_{\text{current}}, \dots, w_{\text{final}}\}$ are updated using the ratio of the new landmark ceiling to the old landmark ceiling:
$$V_{\text{target}, w}^{\text{new}} = V_{\text{target}, w}^{\text{old}} \times \left( \frac{\text{MRV}^{\text{new}}}{\text{MRV}^{\text{old}}} \right)$$
Rescaled weekly targets are clamped to fall within $[\text{MEV}^{\text{new}}, \text{MRV}^{\text{new}}]$.
Autoregulated Landmark Refinement Engine
Landmarks adjust dynamically based on logged biofeedback. PlanKit processes three weekly input vectors per muscle group: Average Logged RIR Delta ($\Delta \text{RIR}$, the difference between prescribed RIR and actual performed RIR), Perceived Soreness Score ($S$, an integer scale from $0$ to $4$ where $0 =$ None, $1 =$ Healed rapidly, $2 =$ Healed just in time, $3 =$ Sore upon entering session, $4 =$ Severe debilitating soreness), and Performance Trend ($\Delta \text{e1RM}$, the percentage change in estimated $1\text{RM}$ on key indicator lifts over a 2-week rolling window).

Weekly Target and Intra-Session Set Split Algorithm
Weekly Volume Ramp Profile
A mesocycle spans $4\text{--}6$ active training weeks followed by a $1$-week deload. PlanKit uses a linear-step progression from MEV to MRV across accumulating weeks $W_1 \dots W_N$:
$$V_{\text{target}}(W_k) = \text{MEV} + \left( \frac{k - 1}{N - 1} \right) \times (\text{MRV} - \text{MEV})$$
For a standard 5-week accumulation mesocycle ($N=5$) with $\text{MEV} = 10.0$ and $\text{MRV} = 18.0$:
Week 1: $10.0$ sets ($\text{MEV}$)
Week 2: $12.0$ sets
Week 3: $14.0$ sets ($\text{MAV}$ mid-point)
Week 4: $16.0$ sets
Week 5: $18.0$ sets ($\text{MRV}$ functional overreach)
Week 6 (Deload): $5.0$ sets ($50\%$ of $\text{MEV}$ / Maintenance Volume)
Session Allocation and Set Splitting
Given a weekly volume target $V_{\text{target}}$ for a muscle group, and $M$ planned session slots hitting that muscle group across the week (for example, $M=2$ for a Push/Pull/Legs split repeated twice weekly):
The engine calculates the base session target via:
$$V_{\text{session, base}} = \frac{V_{\text{target}}}{M}$$
Within a single session containing $P$ primary compound slots and $I$ secondary isolation slots targeting muscle group $m$, primary compound slots are allocated a baseline of $2\text{--}4$ sets per slot. Secondary isolation slots are allocated remaining required sets to satisfy $V_{\text{session, base}}$.
Every included exercise slot must be assigned $\ge 2.0$ sets. If assigned sets $< 2.0$, the engine drops the lowest priority isolation slot and reallocates sets to primary slots to eliminate ineffective 1-set slots. Furthermore, no single muscle group may exceed $8.0$ direct sets ($10.0$ total effective sets) in a single workout session. Excessive sets overflow into a newly synthesized session slot or increase frequency across the week.
Time Budget Constraints and Multipliers
Sessions are constrained by explicit time budgets ($45$, $60$, or $75$ minutes). PlanKit calculates total session time ($T_{\text{session}}$) deterministically:
$$T_{\text{session}} = \sum_{j} \left( S_j \times t_{\text{rep}} + (S_j - 1) \times t_{\text{rest}, j} \right) + t_{\text{warmup}}$$
In this calculation, $t_{\text{rep}} = 40 \text{ seconds}$ per set, $t_{\text{rest, compound}} = 180 \text{ seconds}$, $t_{\text{rest, isolation}} = 90 \text{ seconds}$, and $t_{\text{warmup}} = 300 \text{ seconds}$.

Readiness Integration
Readiness scoring acts as an inline scalar ($\beta_{\text{readiness}} \in [0.5, 1.1]$) multiplying the planned session set allocation:
$$S_{\text{prescribed}} = \text{ROUND}\left( S_{\text{planned}} \times \beta_{\text{readiness}} \right)$$
The unit multiplied by readiness is strictly the planned intra-session set count for that specific day's workout. It does not rewrite the weekly base landmark ledger ($V_{\text{target}}$).

Execution Algorithms
Standard Double Progression maintains load static ($W$) while expanding repetitions performed across working sets until reaching the upper boundary of the rep range ($R_{\text{max}}$) at target RIR. Once all prescribed sets hit $R_{\text{max}}$, the working load increments by Load Step Size ($\Delta W$) for the subsequent session, resetting reps to $R_{\text{min}}$.
Rep-First Step Progression keeps load static ($W$) while adding reps set-by-set. When Set 1 reaches $R_{\text{max}}$, reps are added to Set 2 in subsequent sessions. Load increases only when every set achieves $R_{\text{max}}$ at or below prescribed RIR.

Experience-Tier Dynamic Adjustments
Experience level modifies progression parameters directly rather than relying solely on volume changes:
Novices utilize a fixed weekly load increment (+2.5 kg on compounds weekly) with wide rep windows ($6\text{--}10$ reps), with stall detection set to 3 consecutive missed exposures. Intermediates employ standard double progression as specified above, with stall detection set to 2 consecutive exposures. Advanced lifters utilize micro-periodized wave loading (alternating high-load/low-rep and low-load/high-rep sessions across the week) with micro-scaled load increments ($+1.0 \text{ kg}$) and a stall threshold of 2 exposures.
Estimated $1\text{RM}$ Initialization and Historical Time Decay
When initializing working weights for a new exercise, PlanKit evaluates logged historical data using the Epley formula:
$$\text{e1RM} = w \times \left(1 + \frac{r}{30}\right)$$
In this equation, $w$ is weight lifted and $r$ is repetitions completed. To account for strength decay during layoffs or unperformed exercises, a time-decay factor ($\lambda = 0.005 \text{ day}^{-1}$) is applied to historical $\text{e1RM}$ logs older than 14 days:
$$\text{e1RM}_{\text{adjusted}} = \text{e1RM}_{\text{logged}} \times e^{-\lambda \cdot (t - 14)}$$
Here, $t$ represents days elapsed since the historic lift was logged. Initial working load ($W_{\text{init}}$) for a target rep count $R_{\text{target}}$ and target $RIR_{\text{target}}$ is calculated deterministically:
$$W_{\text{init}} = \frac{\text{e1RM}_{\text{adjusted}}}{1 + \frac{R_{\text{target}} + RIR_{\text{target}}}{30}}$$

Evidence Appendix and Open Decisions
Literature Synthesis
The mechanics of the Volume Ruler engine are grounded in foundational exercise science literature:
Volume dose-response research demonstrates that hypertrophy exhibits a graded relationship with training volume up to an upper recovery ceiling, beyond which diminishing returns occur. The $10\text{--}20$ set weekly range represents the optimal Maximum Adaptive Volume (MAV) window for most muscle groups.
Multi-level meta-regressions confirm that fractional set counting (assigning $0.5$ credit to synergists) provides a superior statistical fit for muscle growth models compared to direct-only or binary set models.
mechanical tension and motor unit recruitment require working sets to terminate within $0\text{--}4 \text{ RIR}$. Sets completed at $>4 \text{ RIR}$ fail to provide sufficient hypertrophic stimulus and are excluded from volume ledgers.
Neuromuscular fatigue studies demonstrate severe diminishing returns when exceeding $6\text{--}10$ sets for a single muscle group in a single training session, supporting intra-session set allocation caps.
