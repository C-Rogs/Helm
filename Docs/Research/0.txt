Programming Specification for Helm’s Adaptive Training Engine: Architecture, Session Composition, and Autoregulation Schema
1. Executive Summary
The fundamental architectural flaw in Helm’s legacy training engine stems from an oversimplified volume allocation model within the PrescriptionEngine. While weekly hard-set calculations managed by PlanKit accurately compute volume landmarks such as Minimum Effective Volume (MEV) and Maximum Recoverable Volume (MRV), collapsing a muscle group’s total weekly volume into a single selected exercise per session creates severely compromised training programs. For example, when a Pull session prescribes eight weekly sets of back work and six weekly sets of bicep work across two weekly exposures, the legacy engine assigns four sets to a single back exercise (e.g., Lat Pulldown) and three sets to a single bicep movement (e.g., Standing Barbell Curl).
This approach breaks down in real-world personal training environments due to four major physiological and biomechanical factors:
Intra-Session Stimulus Decay: Concentrating four to six consecutive hard sets onto a single movement vector produces acute localized fatigue that dramatically degrades the stimulus-to-fatigue ratio (SFR) of subsequent sets.
Anatomical and Sub-Division Neglect: Complex skeletal muscle structures—such as the latissimus dorsi, trapezius, and deltoids—comprise distinct fiber orientations and regional innervation patterns that require varied movement vectors (e.g., vertical pulling, horizontal rowing, and isolation extension) to achieve comprehensive muscular development.
Connective Tissue Micro-Trauma: Repeatedly loading a single joint angle under high volume increases cumulative mechanical stress on periarticular structures, elevating overuse injury risk compared to distributing volume across complementary movement patterns.
Session Density and Time Constraints: Single-exercise set dumping leads to unrealistic session flow and density, failing to reflect professional personal training standards.
To eliminate this systemic defect without compromising offline deterministic calculation, Helm must transition from a Muscle-First Set Allocator to a dual-layer Movement Pattern Session Composer (MPSC) coupled with a Dynamic Volume Allocation & Autoregulation System (DVAAS). Under this updated architecture, weekly hard-set targets calculated by PlanKit are dynamically mapped across mandatory movement pattern slots structured by session duration budgets, equipment constraints, and movement selection preferences.

Domain Functional Specifications
Program Parameters (Top-Level Architecture)
Defines structural training components including Primary Split Strategy (PPL, Upper_Lower, Full_Body), Microcycle Frequency ($3$ to $6$ days per week), and Target Session Duration ($30$, $45$, $60$, $75+$ minutes). Modifying any Program Parameter forces a complete re-compilation of the active mesocycle schedule.
Methodology Preferences
Controls engine heuristics within established program structures. Key variables include Movement Selection Bias (Stretch_Focused, SFR_Optimized, Compound_Heavy), Proximity-to-Failure Policy (Target_RIR_Ramp, Fixed_RIR), Progression Schemes (Double_Progression, Dynamic_Double_Progression), and Volume Aggressiveness Multipliers ($0.8\times$ to $1.2\times$ baseline velocity).
Operational Constraints
Acts as a dynamic filter on the exercise database prior to movement pattern slotting. Includes Equipment Availability Bitmasks (Barbell, Dumbbell, Cable, Plate_Loaded, Bodyweight) and Joint/Pain Constraints (Flexion_Intolerant_Lumbar, Subacromial_Impingement, Patellar_Tendinopathy).
User Experience Prior
Replaces static experience scalars with an Individualized Historical Baseline ($V_{base}$) derived from user-reported training volume over the preceding eight weeks. This value initializes the starting microcycle volume budget.
Educational & Scientific Library
Houses verified exercise physiology documentation. These assets configure zero engine code directly; instead, they provide structured context for LLM Coach interactions, enabling accurate, evidence-backed user feedback.

Two-Exercise Session Validation Rules
A session containing only two exercise slots is classified as an engine Defect unless one of the following validated system conditions is active:
Time Budget Limit: User active time budget is set to $30\text{ minutes}$.
Readiness Depressed: Morning Readiness (ARC) score falls below $0.40$, triggering an automated volume reduction to prevent excessive fatigue.
Deload Phase: Active microcycle phase is flagged as Deload, reducing targets to Maintenance Volume ($MV$, $\sim 50\%$ of baseline volume).
Acute Joint Constraint: Active pain constraints exclude $\ge 50\%$ of candidate movement patterns for the targeted muscle groups.
Overlap and Synergy Set Accounting Logic
To track fatigue and volume accurately without double-counting set totals, Helm utilizes a dual-tier set credit system:
Direct Set Credit (1.0): Granted to primary mover muscles in a movement pattern (e.g., Barbell Incline Bench Press grants 1.0 set credit to Pectoralis Major).
Synergy Set Credit (0.5): Granted to primary synergist muscles receiving substantial mechanical tension and proximity to failure (e.g., Barbell Incline Bench Press grants 0.5 set credit to Anterior Deltoid and Triceps Brachii).
Stabilizer Credit (0.0): Secondary postural stabilizers receive 0.0 set credit toward hypertrophic volume landmarks, but their mechanical contribution is logged for systemic fatigue calculations (e.g., Erector Spinae during Barbell Rows).
MOVEMENT INPUT: Barbell Incline Bench Press (3 Hard Sets Performed)
  |
  +---> Pectoralis Major (Primary Mover)  --> 1.0 Credit x 3 Sets = 3.0 Direct Sets Logged
  +---> Anterior Deltoid (Synergist)      --> 0.5 Credit x 3 Sets = 1.5 Synergy Sets Logged
  +---> Triceps Brachii (Synergist)       --> 0.5 Credit x 3 Sets = 1.5 Synergy Sets Logged
  +---> Erector Spinae (Stabilizer)       --> 0.0 Credit x 3 Sets = 0.0 Hypertrophy Credit (Fatigue Only)
Deterministic Session Composer Algorithm
Algorithm: DeterministicSessionComposer
Input:
    TargetMuscles: Array of Muscle Enums for active day split
    WeeklySetBudgets: Map<Muscle, Int> derived from PlanKit MEV->MRV models
    RemainingSessionsInMicrocycle: Map<Muscle, Int>
    TimeBudgetMinutes: Int
    EquipmentMask: Bitmask
    PainConstraints: Array<Constraint>
    UserSelectionBias: SelectionBias Enum

Output:
    ResolvedSessionPlan: Array<ExerciseAssignment>

Begin:
    1. Set MaxSlots, MaxSets, MaxSetsPerSlot = LookupDurationRules(TimeBudgetMinutes)
    2. CandidatePatterns = ResolvePatternSlotsForSplit(TargetMuscles)
    
    3. Filter CandidatePatterns:
         For each pattern in CandidatePatterns:
             If PainConstraints.Excludes(pattern):
                 Remove pattern from CandidatePatterns
                 
    4. Compute DailyMuscleTargets:
         For each muscle in TargetMuscles:
             DailyMuscleTargets[muscle] = Ceiling(WeeklySetBudgets[muscle] / RemainingSessionsInMicrocycle[muscle])
             
    5. Initialize SessionPlan = Empty Array
    6. Set RemainingSessionSets = MaxSets
    
    7. Sort CandidatePatterns by Priority (Primary Compounds -> Secondary Compounds -> Isolation)
    
    8. For each slot in CandidatePatterns:
         If RemainingSessionSets <= 0 OR SessionPlan.Length >= MaxSlots:
             Break Loop
             
         Set TargetMuscle = slot.PrimaryMuscle
         If DailyMuscleTargets[TargetMuscle] <= 0:
             Continue Loop
             
         Set EligibleExercises = QueryDatabase(slot.Pattern, EquipmentMask, PainConstraints)
         If EligibleExercises is Empty:
             Continue Loop
             
         Set SelectedExercise = RankAndSelect(EligibleExercises, UserSelectionBias)
         
         // Calculate set assignment
         Set AllocatedSets = Minimum(DailyMuscleTargets[TargetMuscle], MaxSetsPerSlot)
         Set AllocatedSets = Minimum(AllocatedSets, RemainingSessionSets)
         
         If AllocatedSets > 0:
             Append ExerciseAssignment(SelectedExercise, AllocatedSets, slot.Pattern) to SessionPlan
             
             // Deduct Direct Volume Target
             DailyMuscleTargets[TargetMuscle] -= AllocatedSets
             RemainingSessionSets -= AllocatedSets
             
             // Apply Synergy Credits
             For each synergist in SelectedExercise.SynergistList:
                 If DailyMuscleTargets.Contains(synergist.Muscle):
                     DailyMuscleTargets[synergist.Muscle] -= (AllocatedSets * 0.5)

    9. Return SessionPlan
End
4. Volume & Experience Model
Critique of Static Experience Multipliers
Helm’s legacy volume seeding model applied fixed scalars ($0.85\times$ Novice, $1.0\times$ Intermediate, $1.1\times$ Advanced) to static baseline landmarks. This approach presents significant physiological limitations. Training experience does not scale hypertrophic volume landmarks linearly. Novice lifters generate low fatigue per set and achieve robust muscle growth from low absolute set counts ($6$–$10$ sets/muscle/week), but possess limited work capacity. Conversely, highly advanced lifters recruit motor units more efficiently and produce far greater mechanical force per set, generating substantial localized micro-trauma and neurological fatigue. Consequently, an advanced athlete may hit their Maximum Recoverable Volume (MRV) at a lower set count for heavy compound lifts than an intermediate lifter.
Helm replaces fixed experience scalars with an Individualized Historical Baseline ($V_{base}$) model. Initial microcycle set targets are computed from the user's self-reported weekly hard sets over the preceding eight weeks, clamped within evidence-based starting boundaries:
$$V_{start} = \text{Clamp}\left(V_{reported\_historical\_average}, \, 8, \, 15\right)$$

Mid-Program Experience Multiplier Change Policy
If a user modifies their self-selected experience level mid-mesocycle, PlanKit must not re-seed active microcycle set volumes. Modifying landmarks mid-block disrupts progressive overload progression and invalidates dynamic autoregulation data. Changes to experience settings are locked and only applied during the compilation of the subsequent mesocycle block.
Algorithmic Landmark Refinement Matrix
At the conclusion of each microcycle, PlanKit refines individual muscle group MEV and MRV parameters using an empirical evaluation matrix combining logged Soreness (1–4), Performance Progression (1–4), and Average Weekly Readiness (ARC).
Soreness Metrics (S):
  1: No soreness / fully recovered > 48h prior to next exposure
  2: Recovered just in time for next session
  3: Residual soreness present during subsequent session
  4: Severe soreness impairing movement performance

Performance Progression (P):
  1: Exceeded load/rep targets with reserve (+2 RIR margin)
  2: Met load/rep targets at prescribed RIR exactly
  3: Failed target reps by 1-2 reps or missed target RIR
  4: Performance regression (failed targets by > 2 reps across sets)

Special Population Adaptations
Masters Athletes ($\ge 45$ Years): Connective tissue remodeling rates are prolonged relative to muscular recovery. PlanKit caps progressive volume accumulation at $+1\text{ set/week}$, applies a $0.85\times$ scalar to calculated MRV caps, and mandates a recovery deload every 4th microcycle.
Layoff Returnees ($\ge 3$ Weeks Un-trained): Muscular adaptation baseline resets while vulnerability to exercise-induced muscle damage increases. The engine sets Microcycle 1 volume to Maintenance Volume ($MV \approx 6\text{ sets/week}$), progressing to $MEV$ in Microcycle 2.
High Stress / Low Sleep (ARC $< 0.45$ 7-Day Average): Systemic recovery capacity is compromised. PlanKit clamps peak mesocycle volume to $MEV + 2\text{ sets}$, overriding progressive set ramps until systemic readiness metrics normalize.
Overreaching Detection & MRV Threshold Enforcements
To prevent overtraining, PlanKit establishes two MRV boundary limits:
Soft Warning Cap ($MRV_{soft} = 0.90 \times MRV$): The LLM Coach alerts the athlete that recovery capacity is reaching saturation. Progressive volume additions are halved.
Hard Operational Cap ($MRV_{hard} = 1.00 \times MRV$): The engine strictly rejects set additions beyond this ceiling regardless of user adjustments.
Overreaching Alarm: If a muscle group logs a Performance Rating of $4$ for two consecutive microcycles while operating at or above $MRV_{soft}$, the engine terminates the active mesocycle and schedules a 7-day reactive deload.

Autoregulation must not depress training volume so frequently that an athlete spends consecutive weeks training below MEV.
$$\text{Floor Constraint: If } \sum_{i=1}^{2} V_{\text{actual}, i} < 2 \times MEV \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{Trigger Mandatory Plan Realignment}$$
If readiness gating depresses total weekly volume below MEV for two consecutive microcycles, the engine locks session set allocations at exact MEV baseline targets and enforces an increased proximity-to-failure buffer ($\ge 3$ RIR). This preserves hypertrophy-stimulating volume while preventing excessive central fatigue accumulation.

Deload Execution Triggers
Deload microcycles are governed by two distinct mechanisms within PlanKit:
Scheduled Deloads: Standard mesocycle periodization schedules a deload automatically every 4th to 6th microcycle based on user training age and recovery capacity.
Reactive Deloads: Triggered immediately at the next microcycle boundary if any of the following conditions occur:
Performance Failure: Estimated 1RM drops by $>5\%$ across primary movements over two consecutive sessions.
Unresolved Soreness: Soreness rating of $4/4$ persists for $>72\text{ hours}$ across two or more primary muscle groups.
Readiness Depression: ARC score remains below $0.40$ for three consecutive days.

Red-Flag Boundaries & Medical Referrals
Non-Diagnostic Imperative: Helm does not provide medical diagnoses. The user interface strictly avoids clinical labels such as "Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome" or "Rotator Cuff Tendinitis." Pain inputs are categorized strictly as functional movement constraints (e.g., Knee_Flexion_Pain_Gated).
Automated Care Referrals: If a user logs a Pain Severity Rating $\ge 7/10$ during session tracking, or if joint pain persists for $>3$ consecutive weeks despite pattern substitutions, the engine locks progression for the affected muscle groups and displays a prompt advising evaluation by a licensed physical therapist or sports physician.
Asymmetry & Physical Therapy Integration
Rehabilitation exercises prescribed by physical therapists are integrated into session plans via Unilateral Rehab Slots:
Physical therapy exercises are flagged as Rehab_Accessory within the exercise database.
Volume completed within Rehab_Accessory slots is tracked separately and does not count toward hypertrophic MRV limits.
If a user specifies a limb strength asymmetry $>15\%$, PlanKit automatically assigns $1.5\times$ set volume to the lagging limb using unilateral movement variants, matching working loads to the capacity of the weaker side.

9. Evidence Appendix & Nutrition Interface
Nutrition Interface & Training Periodization
Nutritional state directly impacts muscular recovery capacity, altering training engine parameters across distinct dietary phases:
Caloric Deficit (Fat Loss / Cut Phase): Systemic recovery capacity and muscle protein synthesis rates are attenuated. PlanKit lowers default MRV caps by $15$–$25\%$, halts progressive volume ramps once $MAV$ baseline is reached, and prioritizes absolute intensity of effort ($\le 2$ RIR) over high volume accumulation to preserve lean mass.
Caloric Surplus (Hypertrophy / Gain Phase): Glycogen availability and systemic recovery capacities are optimized. PlanKit enables aggressive volume progression rates ($+2\text{ to }+3\text{ sets/week}$) up to the full $MRV_{hard}$ ceiling.
Low Energy Availability (LEA) & REDs Risk Signals: If an athlete logs persistent performance declines across $>2$ microcycles alongside low ARC readiness, elevated muscle soreness ($4/4$), and suppressed resting heart rate metrics, the engine flags potential LEA risk. The system restricts session volume to $MV$, locks RIR at $\ge 3$, and prompts the user to evaluate their nutritional intake.
